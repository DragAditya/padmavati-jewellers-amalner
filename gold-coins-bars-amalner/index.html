The short answer

Padmavati Jewellers sells 24K gold coins and small bars in Saraf Bazar, Amalner. Coins are 999 purity and come sealed. Because there is no design work, the making charge on a coin is the lowest in the shop, which is why people buy them for Dhanteras, weddings and saving.

Why people buy coins instead of jewellery

A bangle carries the price of the gold plus the price of the work. A coin carries the price of the gold and very little else. If the aim is to hold gold rather than to wear it, the coin is the cheaper way to do it — and that is the honest reason we sell more coins at Dhanteras than at any other time.

What to check on a coin

  1. The purity mark. 24K coins should be 999. Anything less is not 24K, whatever the packet says.
  2. The seal. Buy it sealed, with the weight and purity printed on the packing.
  3. The weight on the scale. Watch it weighed, and match it to the packet.
  4. The bill. A coin without a bill is hard to sell back or use for a loan later. Ask for one.

Common weights

Coins are normally sold in round weights. Ask what is in stock on the day — the exact denominations change with supply, and we will tell you what we actually have rather than promise a weight we cannot give you.

Dhanteras and wedding season

Demand spikes in the days before Dhanteras and through the wedding months. The gold rate is not what moves most on those days — it is availability and making charge. If you know you want a specific weight, ask us ahead of the festival and we will keep it aside.

Selling a coin back

We buy coins back at the day's rate for their purity, the same way as any old gold. Keep the packet and the bill: a sealed coin with its original packing is simpler to value than a loose one. How old gold is valued.

Are coins cheaper than jewellery?
