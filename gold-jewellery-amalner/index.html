How the price of a gold piece is worked out

Gold is sold on four numbers. Knowing them is the difference between a fair bill and an expensive one, so we put all four on the paper before you decide.

  1. Net weight. Gross weight minus the weight of stones, beads or enamel. This is the weight you actually pay gold for.
  2. Rate for the karat. The day's 22K, 24K or 18K rate per gram, multiplied by net weight.
  3. Making charge. Our labour on the piece. Depending on the design it is a percentage of the gold value, a flat figure per gram, or a wastage percentage — we tell you which one applies, and the figure, before you commit.
  4. GST, only if you want a GST invoice. 3% is added on the gold value plus making charge, and printed as a separate line. Without the invoice, the quoted price is what you pay.

If you want to compare two shops, ask each one for the making charge in rupees on the same piece of the same weight. That single number is where shops actually differ.

Buying gold safely, wherever you buy it

Three habits protect you in any shop, including this one. Ask for the HUID and check it on the BIS Care app. Watch the scale with the stone weight removed. And keep the bill — it is the document a buyback, an exchange or a gold loan will ask for later.

Gold questions we get asked

What is the gold rate in Amalner today?

Gold in Amalner follows the Mumbai bullion rate plus local making charges, and moves daily. Call the shop on the number at the top of this page for the current 24K, 22K and 18K rate per gram.

Is 22K or 24K better for a wedding set?

22K (916) is the usual choice for bridal jewellery in Maharashtra. It keeps the deep colour but is harder than 24K, so clasps, bangles and daily-wear pieces hold their shape. 24K is normally bought as coins or bars.

How is the making charge calculated?

It depends on the design. For some pieces it is a percentage of the gold value, for others a flat amount per gram, and for traditional work a wastage percentage. We tell you which applies, and the rupee figure, before you buy.

Can I exchange old gold for new jewellery?

Yes. We test and weigh the old gold in front of you, price it at the day's rate for its purity, and adjust that value against the new piece. You can also take the value as cash instead.

Can you make a piece from a photo or sketch?

Yes, for gold and for bridal sets. Bring the photo or drawing, and we will confirm the weight, purity, making charge and delivery date with you before starting.

Do you give a GST bill?

Yes, if you ask for one. Tell us when you buy or when you collect an order. A GST invoice adds 3% on the gold value plus making charge, and it is printed with the weight and karat.
