Silver utensils and pooja items

Arti plates, diyas and gangajal pots — bought for a housewarming, a naming, or a wedding list.

The short answer

Padmavati Jewellers sells silver utensils and pooja items in Saraf Bazar, Amalner, including arti plates, small diyas, gangajal pots, spoons and silver coins. They are bought for housewarmings, naming ceremonies, pooja and wedding return gifts, and are priced by weight plus making.

What people buy, and for what

Pooja silver is bought to a date, not on impulse. That changes what matters: the piece has to be the right size for the thali, the right weight for the budget, and ready on the day. Tell us the date first and we will tell you honestly whether it is comfortable.

Arti plates and thalis

Small and full-size arti plates, plain or with engraving. Most families buy these for a housewarming or before a first pooja in a new flat.

Diyas and lamps

Silver diyas in the sizes used for daily pooja and for festival evenings. Also small standing lamps, which are the common gift for a wedding in the family.

Gangajal pots and kalash

Small lidded pots for gangajal and pooja water, and kalash for a housewarming or a satyanarayan pooja.

Spoons and small utensils

Silver spoons, small bowls and sets — the traditional gift for a first meal, a naming ceremony, or a grandchild's first solid food.

Silver coins

Silver coins for pooja, for gifting, and for placing in a new safe or a new shop on opening day. Sold single or in quantity.

Buying for a wedding list

Return gifts are usually the largest single order we get for silver. Bring the count, the budget per piece and the wedding date. We confirm the piece and the price per piece before the order starts, and you collect it before the function rather than on the morning of it.

Purity and weight

Utensils and pooja items are not always the same alloy as jewellery silver, because a plate that holds water or a lamp that sits over a flame has different requirements. Ask which purity a piece is — we will tell you, and it is marked on the item. Everything is priced by weight plus making, the same way as our jewellery.

Care

Silver darkens with air and with the sulphur in some foods. Keep pooja pieces dry, wipe them after use rather than leaving water on them, and bring them in for a polish when they dull. Do not scrub an engraved piece with an abrasive — it wears the pattern away.

Pooja silver questions

Do you sell silver pooja items in Amalner?

Yes — arti plates, diyas, gangajal pots, kalash, spoons and silver coins. They are priced by weight plus making, the same way as our jewellery.

Can I order silver for wedding return gifts?

Yes, and it is one of the larger orders we handle. Bring the count, the budget per piece and the wedding date, and we will confirm the price before starting.

How far in advance should I order?

For a handful of pieces, a few days is usually enough. For a full wedding list, come at least a few weeks ahead so there is no rush on the last morning.

Is the silver marked with its purity?

The purity is marked on the piece. Ask us which it is before you buy — pooja silver and jewellery silver are not always the same alloy, and the difference matters for a piece that holds water or sits near a flame.
